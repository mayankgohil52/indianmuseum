About the National Museum, New Delhi

Established in 1949 and inaugurated at its grand Janpath building in 1960, the National Museum in New Delhi is the premier cultural institution of India.

Spread across three floors with more than 20 specialized galleries, the museum safeguards priceless treasures ranging from prehistoric stone tools and Indus Valley seals to sacred Buddhist relics, royal weaponry, and ornate gold jewelry.

Iconic Masterpieces at the Museum

World-renowned treasures housed inside the National Museum, New Delhi

Harappan Gallery (Ground Floor)

The Dancing Girl & Indus Relics (2500 BCE)

Cast in bronze using the lost-wax technique at Mohenjo-daro, this 4,500-year-old statuette is the crown jewel of the Indus Valley Civilization gallery.

Bronze Gallery (Ground Floor)

Chola Bronze Nataraja (12th Century CE)

An extraordinary South Indian bronze masterpiece depicting Lord Shiva as the Cosmic Dancer surrounded by a ring of sacred flames.

Miniature Gallery (First Floor)

Mughal & Rajasthani Miniatures

Home to over 17,000 miniature paintings brushed with natural mineral colors and pure gold leaf across Mughal, Pahari, and Deccani schools.

Visitor Guidelines & FAQs

Click on a question below to view official museum guidelines

Which is the nearest Delhi Metro station to the National Museum? +
The nearest metro stations are Central Secretariat (Yellow & Violet Lines) and Udyog Bhawan (Yellow Line), both within a 5-minute walk from Janpath Road.
What are the entry ticket charges for Indian students and adults? +
Entry for Indian adults is ₹20, Foreign nationals is ₹500 (including Audio Guide), and entry is FREE for school/college students carrying a valid institutional ID card.
Are cameras and mobile photography permitted inside? +
Still photography without flash or tripods is permitted in most galleries for personal use. Flash photography is strictly prohibited to protect ancient manuscripts and paintings.
